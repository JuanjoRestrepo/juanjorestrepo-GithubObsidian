RPA is software. It goes through all the software development stages, including testing. RPA testing deals with issues that are typically discovered in production, but aims to do it as early as possible in the process.

## So, let’s ask the question, what breaks our robots?   

Basically, there are 3 main issue groups that might break our robot in a production environment:  

1. The first problem group is centered around application changes. These changes are typically introduced through Software Development by adding new functionalities, new controls, or new interfaces to the applications that are part of the automated business process.   
	If we do not incorporate such changes into our RPA process in a timely manner our robots will fail during production.   

2. The second problem group arises from environmental issues related to performance, system updates introduced by IT Operations via security patches, or browser updates rolled out directly into the production environment.   

3. The third problem group is related to automation issues itself, consisting in poor object recognition or missing recovery handling.   

Failure to mitigate the risks can lead to higher maintenance costs, slows the automations scaling efforts as well as can cause business disruption.
