Every IBM Garage starts with questions: 
- What are your strategic imperatives? 
- What are the important business and operational challenges that the Garage is hoping to solve? 
- What parts of the business do you want to focus on?

Co-create is the first phase in the IBM Garage

### **1. Co-create**
---
Its goal is to answer these questions and make sure you're solving the right problems and pursuing the most valuable solutions for your end users.

There are three elements to the Co-create phase: 
- Understanding and defining potential business opportunities.
- Aligning on and prioritizing the most important ones to address.
- Establishing and validating a vision for a solution and getting ready to start building.

The intent as you move through Co-create is to form firm hypotheses about the business challenges you're facing, align on which ones are most important to solve, and develop and validate a vision for how you might address each.

NOTE:
IBM Garage is underpinned by a **flexible framework**. 
A squad could execute Co-create during a **two-day workshop, or it could dedicate multiple sprints to exploring and prioritizing opportunities**. 

It all **depends on the type of challenges you're faced with** and the scope and timeline of your Garage.

#### Opportunities
Co-create is to understand the potential opportunities your Garage can pursue.

Opportunities is a word you’ll hear a lot in the IBM Garage. An opportunity is a business challenge reframed. Most clients come to IBM with a sense of the challenges they want to tackle. Garages take those challenges and apply a proactive approach to defining and solving them.

It’s our job to make sure everyone deeply understands the challenges and helps decide which ones to pursue first.

Depending on the type of challenge you’re faced with, you might consider:
- Framing sessions to understand stakeholder concerns.
- Persona generation to empathize with user wants and needs.
- Pain point and opportunity mapping to highlight issues in the current state.
- Design research sessions to deeply understand your users.
- Root cause analyses to define underlying issues.
- Architectural analyses to understand any and all potential developmental challenges.
- Value Tree creation to determine where and how your opportunity is impacting the underlying business.

Once you deeply understand the available opportunities, the next step is to **prioritize them and decide where to start.**

Now that you know which opportunity to pursue, the next step is to begin to **craft a vision for a potential solution**. At this stage, you don’t need to define what the entire solution might look like; your goal is just to ensure that you’re moving in the right direction.

You have an idea about what you could build that might provide value. **Think about the smallest thing you can build to confirm your hypothesis about your potential solution.**

In some cases that might be a **hand-drawn wireframe, or a storyboard**. In others, it might be **a simple data model**. 

**What’s the leanest thing you can make to get feedback on and ensure you’re moving in the right direction?**
